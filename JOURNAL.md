# 🌲 Smart Forest Fire Alert and Autonomous Extinguisher System

**Project Name:** Smart Forest Fire Alert and Autonomous Extinguisher System  
**Project Creator:** Ritik Prajapat  
**Total Development Time:** 52.7 Hours  
**Platform:** Arduino Uno  
**Submission:** Forge Hack Club

---

## Stage 1: Idea Generation and Project Planning
**Time Spent: 2 Hours**

I started thinking about this project after learning about the harmful effects of forest fires on wildlife and the environment.

I wanted to create a system that could detect fire and smoke and respond without requiring someone to operate every part manually.

During this stage, I developed the initial idea and planned the main features of the project.

**Outcome:**
- Identified the problem.
- Developed the initial project idea.
- Planned the basic functions of the system.

- ---


## Stage 2: Component Research and List Preparation
**Time Spent: 3.9 Hours**

After deciding on the project idea, I researched the electronic components needed to build it.

I considered the role of each component and how the components could work together with an Arduino Uno.

I prepared a component list before starting the physical construction.

**Outcome:**
- Researched suitable electronic components.
- Prepared the component list.
- Developed an initial understanding of the system architecture.

## Stage 4: Component Sketching and Circuit Design
**Time Spent: 6.5 Hours**

Before connecting the components, I worked on sketching their arrangement and preparing the circuit diagram.

This stage helped me understand the connections between the Arduino, sensors, OLED display, servo motor, buzzer, relay module, and other components.

Planning the circuit beforehand was important because incorrect connections could prevent the system from working properly.

**Outcome:**

## Stage 5: Arduino Uno Programming
**Time Spent: 13.6 Hours**

Programming was the most time-consuming stage of my project.

As a beginner in Arduino programming, I needed additional time to understand the code, define the sensor inputs and output pins, and implement the required system behavior.

I worked on the logic for detecting flames and smoke and controlling the connected output devices.

I also worked on displaying the appropriate system status and alert messages on the OLED screen.

This stage taught me that writing code for an electronics project requires both logical thinking and repeated testing.

**Outcome:**
- Developed the Arduino program.
- Worked on the fire-detection and smoke-detection logic.
- Implemented control logic for the connected output devices.
- Improved my understanding of Arduino programming and troubleshooting.

## Stage 6: Circuit Wiring and Troubleshooting
**Time Spent: 8 Hours**

After working on the circuit design and code, I connected the components according to the planned wiring.

During assembly, I encountered five wiring mistakes that required checking and correction.

These mistakes taught me the importance of verifying pin connections, power supplies, ground connections, and signal wires.

Instead of assuming that the circuit was correct, I had to examine the connections and identify possible causes of the problems.

**Outcome:**
- Connected the electronic components.
- Identified and corrected five wiring mistakes.
- Improved my understanding of circuit connections.
- Learned the importance of careful wiring and troubleshooting.

## Stage 7: Working Principle Cross-Checking
**Time Spent: 4 Hours**

Once the circuit and program were prepared, I reviewed the working principle of the complete system.

I checked how the flame sensor and MQ-2 sensor were intended to interact with the Arduino and how the system should respond to different detection conditions.

I also reviewed the expected behavior of the OLED display, buzzer, servo motor, relay module, and water pump.

This stage helped me understand the relationship between the hardware and software.

**Outcome:**
- Reviewed the system's operating logic.
- Cross-checked the expected sensor responses.
- Examined how the output devices should behave.
- Identified areas requiring further hardware verification
